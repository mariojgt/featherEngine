# Feather Engine v0.4.0

This update started with a simple idea: make a short film in Feather, then fix the parts of the engine that got in the way.

**Neon Afterlight** is the result: a 70-second trip through a rainy cyberpunk street, with neon signs, a passing drone, steam, sparks and cooling-water spray. The complete editable project is in the Asset Store. It includes all 16 original assets, ten camera shots, weather cues and the music. Press Play to watch it, or change the lights and cameras to make it your own.

## What changed

- Clearer reflections: missed screen-space traces no longer stretch across surfaces, reflection history resets at camera cuts, and changing lights update reflected color.
- Better water captures: Epic uses full-resolution HDR planar reflections every frame. Rain ripples fade with distance so they do not overwhelm reflected signs.
- Smoother atmosphere: nearby point, spot and area lights color the fog. Narrow beams use deterministic integration, removing the coarse sampling speckles. Sun shadow-depth decoding now matches the renderer.
- More lighting control: rectangular area lights, consistent spotlight aiming and penumbra, shadow settings, exposure controls, live sky lighting, wet materials and weather-driven puddles.
- More reliable cinematic capture: cloth, particles and weather follow simulation time; offline capture waits for assets before recording. Project packages retain their rendering settings.
- Three editable showcase projects: Neon Afterlight, Blackthorn Keep and Last Light, with original assets and scores. The AI assistant can build the templates and use the new lighting controls.

Neon Afterlight v1.1.0 also includes a lit cooling assembly, finer water droplets and a revised close-up. The film is available at 1080p / 24 fps, alongside the editable project and a complete showcase archive.

## Checks and limits

943 unit tests pass. The editor and player build successfully. GPU checks cover fog accuracy, reflection updates, camera cuts, repeated quality changes and real Asset Store imports. The 70-second movie passes a complete decode, and the showcase archive has verified checksums.

This remains an experimental engine. Screen-space reflections depend on visible geometry, planar water uses one dominant height, and local fog does not yet include light-source shadows. The water spray is a particle effect. The offline film is a visual showcase, not a real-time performance benchmark. Epic costs more GPU time and memory.
